Exploratory dementia sensitivity analysis
Author of requested manuscript revisions: Cinja
Specified before fitting the new models, 12 November 2026.

Purpose: assess whether the 2025-minus-2022 swallowing-symptom difference
in adults aged 65+ with stroke history persists after accounting for reported
physician-diagnosed dementia (DEMENEV_A). This is post-hoc, not prespecified
in the original study, and does not establish independence from all causes.

Use unchanged official 2022 and 2025 adult files. Reproduce existing outcome,
age, sex, stroke and full survey-design coding; retain annual weights and
public strata/PSU identifiers unchanged. Include proxy and ambiguous response
status as in the original unrestricted analysis. Codes 7/8/9 are missing.

Fit survey-weighted quasibinomial logistic models among older adults with
known outcome, sex and stroke: outcome ~ year * stroke + agegroup + sex.
Reproduce the published age-sex-adjusted subgroup contrasts first. Then fit
this baseline on dementia-complete cases and add a binary dementia main
effect on exactly those cases. No variable selection or extra interactions.

For all model comparisons use the existing US 2000 age weights crossed with
50% male/female. For the dementia model subdivide each age-sex cell using
the pooled survey-weighted dementia proportion in the dementia-complete
older-adult stroke-model domain, pooling both years and stroke groups.
Use this same fixed hypothetical joint reference for every year and group.
Treat the estimated reference distribution as fixed: confidence intervals
are conditional on it and do not propagate its estimation uncertainty.

Report standardized prevalence, year differences and additive interaction
using analytic marginal gradients and full survey coefficient covariance;
use residual survey degrees of freedom and t-based intervals, logit scale
for prevalence and linear scale for differences. Check gradients numerically,
predictions against predict(), convergence, rank, design df and cell counts.
Report exclusions and compare the baseline on identical complete cases to
separate adjustment effects from changes in sample composition.

This is descriptive sensitivity analysis. Dementia timing/severity and other
neurological causes remain unavailable. Dementia may follow stroke; adjustment
does not identify a causal direct effect. No multiplicity correction. Do not
replace the original primary analysis or confirmed-self-response sensitivity.
