Figure 1. Swallowing problems among adults aged 65 years and older, NHIS 2022 and 2025.

Points indicate survey-weighted, directly age-standardized prevalence estimates of reported swallowing problems during the past 12 months; horizontal lines indicate 95% confidence intervals. Estimates are shown for all adults aged 65 years and older and for groups with and without a reported history of stroke. Displayed event/denominator counts are unweighted: 653/8,485 and 689/8,223 overall in 2022 and 2025, respectively; 98/671 and 108/590 among those with a history of stroke; and 552/7,796 and 580/7,615 among those without a history of stroke. Percentages are weighted and age-standardized and therefore do not equal the ratios of these raw counts. The overall population includes people with unknown stroke status and overlaps with both subgroups. Missing primary-outcome responses are excluded on an outcome-specific basis. Subgroup comparisons are exploratory. The survey years represent repeated cross-sections, not individual trajectories. See the shared methods and limitations below.

Figure 2. Absolute prevalence differences between 2025 and 2022 by outcome and history of stroke.

Points indicate differences in age-standardized prevalence in percentage points (2025 minus 2022); horizontal lines indicate design-based 95% confidence intervals. Positive values indicate higher prevalence in 2025; the vertical zero line denotes no between-year difference. The primary outcome is swallowing problems during the past 12 months. Secondary outcomes are substantial swallowing difficulty (the response “a lot” to the question about the amount of difficulty swallowing) and swallowing problems lasting at least 180 days during the past 12 months. Both secondary prevalence estimates refer to the entire respective population with a valid outcome status, not only to participants reporting swallowing problems. Valid negative responses to the primary question are coded as 0 for the secondary population indicators; missing follow-up responses among participants reporting problems remain missing. Open circles identify the two fragile contrasts in the stroke group: substantial difficulty and duration of at least 180 days. At least one underlying year-by-age cell contains fewer than ten events. This flag is a project-specific rule and does not constitute an official assessment under the NCHS Data Presentation Standards. Secondary outcomes and subgroup analyses are exploratory, and confidence intervals are not adjusted for multiple comparisons. See the shared methods and limitations below.

Figure 3. Sensitivity of the between-year comparison of swallowing problems to survey period and missing responses.

Age-standardized prevalence differences (2025 minus 2022, percentage points) with 95% confidence intervals are shown for all adults aged 65 years and older and for those with a reported history of stroke. The primary analysis of valid primary-outcome responses is compared with restriction to quarters 1–3 and with two opposing extreme assumptions about missing primary-outcome responses. In the lower-bound scenario, all missing responses are treated as yes in 2022 and no in 2025; the upper-bound scenario makes the reverse assignment. The quarterly analysis uses annual weights without additional recalibration and is an exploratory robustness check rather than a fully seasonally adjusted annual comparison. Confidence intervals for the extreme scenarios quantify sampling uncertainty conditional on the specified assignment; they do not form a joint confidence band covering all possible missing-response mechanisms. The scenarios are not plausibility-weighted imputations. They address only missing primary-outcome responses among participants with known age and, where applicable, known stroke status; they do not address unknown age or stroke status, or complete interview nonparticipation. See the shared methods and limitations below.

Shared methods and source statement

The target population is the civilian noninstitutionalized population of the United States aged 65 years and older. Estimates account for the final annual weights (WTFA_A), strata (PSTRAT), and clusters (PPSU) in the NHIS public-use files. The survey design was specified using all adult records, followed by domain analyses. Public-use design identifiers and annual weights were retained without modification for between-year comparisons, as specified in CDC documentation. Variances were estimated by Taylor linearization using the survey package in R. Direct age standardization used the projected 2000 U.S. standard population in the age groups 65–74, 75–84, and ≥85 years, normalized within the population aged 65 years and older (standard population counts: 18,135,514; 12,314,793; and 4,259,173). Standardized prevalence estimates are comparative indices rather than directly observed population proportions. Prevalence intervals are two-sided 95% t-based intervals calculated on the logit scale using the delta method and back-transformed. Intervals for differences use linear contrasts of the full covariance matrix and t quantiles with degrees of freedom from the respective analytic domain.

Findings are based on self- or proxy-reports and do not represent clinically confirmed dysphagia. A history of stroke denotes a report of having previously been told by a health professional that the participant had a stroke; its timing and any causal relationship with swallowing problems are unknown. The 2025 annual file contains 11 sample months with adjusted annual weights following interruptions and shifts in data collection; the December sample month is absent. The sample design also changed in 2025. These differences limit comparability despite consistent question wording. The primary comparison concerns the between-year difference among all adults aged 65 years and older; secondary outcomes, subgroups, and sensitivity analyses are exploratory. Findings do not establish individual change, causal effects, or a continuous temporal trend.

Data source: National Center for Health Statistics, National Health Interview Survey, 2022 and 2025 public-use data; authors’ calculations. Analyses, interpretations, and conclusions do not represent positions of NCHS.

Suggested manuscript placement

Figure 1 is suitable as the main figure. Figures 2 and 3 are suitable for supplementary material or, depending on the manuscript focus and journal requirements, the main text. When using a figure separately, include the relevant shared methods and source statement or refer to a complete methods description.


Figure 4. Stroke-group prevalence differences across the primary and post-hoc analyses.
Points and horizontal lines show absolute differences in reported 12-month swallowing-problem prevalence (2025 minus 2022, percentage points) and design-based 95% confidence intervals among adults aged 65 years and older with a reported history of stroke. Direct age-standardized estimates use the projected U.S. 2000 standard population in the age groups 65-74, 75-84 and 85 years or older. Model-based adjusted estimates additionally standardize to a fixed 50:50 male:female distribution within age groups using a survey-weighted logistic model with survey year, stroke history, year-by-stroke interaction, age group and sex. These marginal levels refer to a hypothetical balanced-sex population. Self-response restriction uses conservative year-specific harmonization and excludes proxy and unknown respondent status; it changes the target population. All models retain the original survey weights, strata and clusters. Added analyses were specified after examination of the original results, and all subgroup and sensitivity comparisons are exploratory without multiplicity adjustment. The data are repeated cross-sections and do not establish individual change or causality.
